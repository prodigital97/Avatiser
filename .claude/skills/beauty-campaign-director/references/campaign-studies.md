# Campaign Studies

The evidence behind this skill: 24 documented skincare, cosmetics and beauty campaigns or brand visual systems, gathered from public sources (trade press, award databases, brand pages, agency case studies) in November 2026. Each entry separates **what the sources document** from **what it teaches**, which is our reading and should be treated as interpretation.

This is a qualitative study of documented campaigns. It is not a statistical image analysis, so it gives no percentages. Where sources disagree (dates, results), the entry says so. Agency-reported results are self-reported.

Groups: A. Skin truth and people · B. Proof and science · C. Texture and sensorial · D. Ingredient and origin · E. Ritual and object · F. Shade range and colour cosmetics · G. India.

## A. Skin truth and people

**1. Dove — Campaign for Real Beauty (Unilever / Ogilvy Düsseldorf & London, 2004–)**
- Documented: real women instead of professional models, photographed by Rankin without airbrushing; built on a survey of women's self-image. Launch timing differs between sources (February vs September 2004).
- Teaches: unretouched skin can *be* the concept. The photographer's instruction to stop styling and let the subjects be themselves is the move.
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Dove_Beauties), [ARF/Ogilvy case study](https://thearf-org-unified-admin.s3.amazonaws.com/ARF%20Ogilvy%20Award%20Case%20Studies/2007%20ARF%20David%20Ogivly%20Award%20CS/Dove_2007_Ogilvy_Case_Study.pdf), [Creative Salon](https://creative.salon/articles/features/history-of-dove)

**2. Glossier — "Skin first, makeup second"**
- Documented: real people over retouched models, dewy natural skin over heavy contouring, close-up product demos (Boy Brow), minimalist pink design system; art director Alena King credited on several campaigns.
- Teaches: show skin at the distance where texture reads, and keep styling minimal so product and skin carry the frame.
- Sources: [Digital Agency Network](https://digitalagencynetwork.com/glossier-marketing-campaigns/), [Into The Gloss](https://intothegloss.com/2015/05/glossier-behind-the-scenes), [Models.com credits](https://models.com/work/glossier-feeling-like-glossier-brand-campaign)

**3. SK-II — Marriage Market Takeover (Forsman & Bodenfors, 2016)**
- Documented: a #ChangeDestiny film taking over Shanghai's marriage market to challenge the "leftover women" label; Cannes Lions Golds, WARC Asia Strategy Grand Prix. The sales impact is from entry materials.
- Teaches: a skincare brand can lead with a social truth and show no product at all. That's a brand film, not a product campaign, so it needs a separate product layer.
- Sources: [Forsman & Bodenfors](https://www.forsman.com/work/marriage-market-takeover/), [Campaign Asia](https://www.campaignasia.com/article/chinas-leftover-women-stand-up-to-marriage-pressure-and-changedestiny/425904), [WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/article/skii_the_marriage_market_takeover/111778)

**4. Rare Beauty — "Rare Beginnings" fragrance (Fred & Farid Paris, 2026)**
- Documented: painterly, still key visuals with the person and product as one composition; the film opens in a cold studio of hard light, glass and steel. No source confirmed the "soft natural light" look often attributed to the brand.
- Teaches: stillness can be a beauty register, with product and face composed as a single image rather than a face shot plus a packshot.
- Sources: [Marketing Dive](https://marketingdive.com/news/rare-beauty-draws-inspiration-from-greek-mythology-for-global-campaign/826225), [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/rare-beauty-rare-beginnings/), [shots](https://shots.net/news/view/rare-beauty-unveils-new-campaign-with-fred-and-farid)

## B. Proof and science

**5. The Ordinary (DECIEM) — clinical identity and text-led OOH**
- Documented: minimal white and cream lab-like labels, a monochrome palette, text-led billboards listing ingredients (2024), and "Periodic Fable" by Uncommon.
- Teaches: for ingredient-literate audiences, the label and the formula are the image. Product on white, typography and restraint.
- Sources: [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2024/05/10/ad-the-day-the-ordinary-s-stripped-back-ooh-prioritizes-science-over-celebrity), [Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/beauty/the-ordinary-taps-ooh-marketing-to-reintroduce-its-science-backed-and-affordable-philosophy/), [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/uncommon-the-ordinary-graphic-design-advertising-project-110225)

**6. CeraVe — "Michael CeraVe" (Ogilvy PR / WPP, Super Bowl 2024)**
- Documented: a staged rumour that Michael Cera made CeraVe, resolved by a boardroom of dermatologists in the Super Bowl spot (dir. Tim & Eric); Cannes Lions Grand Prix. Impression figures are agency-reported.
- Teaches: "developed with dermatologists" is the proof point, and the creative found an entertaining way to land it. Proof brands still need a hook.
- Sources: [WPP](https://www.wpp.com/case-studies/wpp-onefluence-led-by-ogilvy-pr-ceraves-michael-cerave), [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/how-michael-cera-earned-cerave-9-billion-impressions-before-the-super-bowl-kicked-off), [Love the Work](https://lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/michael-cerave-1617206)

**7. Vaseline Verified (Unilever / Ogilvy Singapore, Cannes Lions 2025)**
- Documented: viral Vaseline "hacks" tested in a lab, with passing hacks given a seal and unsafe ones debunked; Health & Wellness and Social & Creator Grand Prix (in 2025, not 2022). Reach figures conflict between sources.
- Teaches: in-use demonstration plus verification. Showing the product doing the thing, then proving it, is a shot list, not just a strategy.
- Sources: [Cosmetics Design Europe](https://www.cosmeticsdesign-europe.com/Article/2025/06/17/vaseline-verified-campaign-wins-grand-prix-at-cannes-lions-2025), [Ogilvy](https://www.ogilvy.com/ideas/vaseline-verified-wins-grand-prix-continuing-ogilvys-social-creator-dominance-for-second), [Unilever](https://www.unilever.com/news/news-search/2025/vaselines-socialfirst-marketing-beauty-hacks-consumer-insights-and-cultural-relevancy/)

**8. Clinique — Irving Penn still lifes (1968–)**
- Documented: from Clinique's launch, Penn shot its advertising as spare, elegant still lifes on plain backdrops; read by art historians as variations on purity and beauty.
- Teaches: the packshot can be the whole campaign when it's composed like art. One product, one idea, controlled light, nothing else.
- Sources: [Art Institute of Chicago — Penn archives](https://archive.artic.edu/irvingpennarchives/advertising), [Wikipedia](https://www.Wikipedia.com/wiki/Irving_Penn)

## C. Texture and sensorial

**9. Rhode — glazed skin (Hailey Bieber as art director)**
- Documented: Bieber credited as art director on campaigns (e.g. Peptide Eye Prep, 2025, photographer Cornelius Kaess); Glazing Mist used black-and-white images of Harris Dickinson with droplets of mist on his skin; Glazing Milk poured over the body; the 2022 debut used stripped-down portraits (Stevie Dance).
- Teaches: name the finish ("glazed") and photograph it literally: droplets, sheen, product on skin. The texture *is* the claim.
- Sources: [Models.com](https://models.com/work/rhode-skin-rhode-peptide-eye-prep-campaign), [The Impression](https://theimpression.com/?p=11658246), [Fashion Gone Rogue](https://fashiongonerogue.com/hailey-bieber-rhode-glazing-milk-campaign)

**10. Laneige — Water Science™**
- Documented: a brand built on "Water Science"; the Water Sleeping Mask in blue packaging; range colour-coding (blue hydration, pink, green Cica); water-molecule-in-a-sphere product visuals.
- Teaches: when the claim is hydration, water and blue carry it. Colour-code by line so each product owns one colour.
- Sources: [Laneige About](https://us.laneige.com/pages/about-us), [Laneige Water Sleeping Mask](https://sg.laneige.com/products/water-sleeping-mask), [Blue Movement](https://www.laneige.com/int/en/brand/blue-movement/)

**11. La Mer — the ocean and kelp**
- Documented: annual Blue Heart jars for World Oceans Day; kelp motifs as a nod to the founder's Giant Sea Kelp; an immersive kelp installation in Shanghai; water and the jar as recurring film imagery.
- Teaches: an origin story (the sea) becomes the brand's single world, applied to packaging, set and motion alike.
- Sources: [Fashion Magazine](https://fashionmagazine.com/beauty-grooming/la-mer-goes-blue-for-world-oceans-day/), [Campaign Asia](https://aws1.campaignasia.com/article/la-mer-transports-visitors-into-ethereal-underwater-experience/468676), [Ads of the World](https://adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/la-mer)

**12. Texture and swatch photography (category practice)**
- Documented: cream and lipstick smears on plain or monochrome grounds; side light for three-dimensional texture; e-commerce guidance favouring accurate pigment, neutral light and no exaggerated saturation.
- Teaches: a texture shot needs raking light for form, and neutral colour for honesty.
- Sources: [Riverflow guide](https://www.riverflow.ai/guides/makeup-product-photography-ideas), [Pixel-shot example](https://pixel-shot.com/en/showcase/i-2853015-0/lipstick-smear-sample-texture)

## D. Ingredient and origin

**13. Kiehl's — ingredient provenance (e.g. glacial glycoprotein)** *(secondary source)*
- Documented: illustrated mountain imagery reinforcing an ingredient's origin, cited as visual proof of where the ingredient comes from.
- Teaches: show the ingredient's world, not a generic nature backdrop.
- Source: [TCF](https://www.tcf.team/blog/beauty-ads)

**14. Innisfree — Jeju**
- Documented: an identity anchored on Jeju island (green tea, volcanic clay, bija, cherry blossom), with own green-tea fields; a 2023–24 rebrand (Tangerine) shifted from naming Jeju ingredients toward "nature's force".
- Teaches: one real place can supply every ingredient and every colour.
- Sources: [Tangerine case study](https://tangerine.net/work/innisfree-branding-and-packaging), [Packaging Insights](https://www.packaginginsights.com/news/innisfree-rebrand-targets-trending-eco-friendly-practices-in-skin-care-and-packaging.html)

**15. Glow Recipe — fruit-led identity**
- Documented: the brand frames each line around one fruit, starting with watermelon. No primary source documents its photo direction.
- Teaches: one ingredient as a recurring visual hero; the fruit is both palette and prop.
- Sources: [Glow Recipe](https://www.glowrecipe.com/pages/about-our-products), [Fashion Magazine](https://fashionmagazine.com/beauty-grooming/watermelon-beauty/)

## E. Ritual and object

**16. Aesop — amber glass still life**
- Documented: amber bottles, stone-toned palettes, restrained lighting and ingredient-led details; Geranium Leaf (the bottle evoking calm at home); Caregiver (APFEL art direction, minimal palette, textured backgrounds); Fragrant Fables (bottles on paper plinths).
- Teaches: a packaging material (amber glass) can be the whole art direction, with a single surface family, soft directional light and few props.
- Sources: [Photoroom review](https://www.photoroom.com/100-best-product-pages/beauty/aesop), [APFEL](https://apracticeforeverydaylife.com/case-studies/aesop), [Kanto](https://kanto.ph/objects/everywhere-we-shoot-aesop-geranium-leaf/), [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/?p=2372840)

## F. Shade range and colour cosmetics

**17. Fenty Beauty — launch (2017)**
- Documented: 40 foundation shades at launch (later 50); a one-minute film and teasers casting women across skin tones; no photographer credit found for the launch.
- Teaches: for complexion, range is the concept. Shoot the spectrum together, never one tone as the default.
- Sources: [TIME](https://time.com/4927962/rihanna-fenty-beauty-makeup-line), [Mintel](https://www.mintel.com/insights/beauty-and-personal-care/fenty-beauty-targeting-women-of-all-colours/), [Essence](https://www.essence.com/syndication/rihanna-fenty-beauty-40-foundation-shades)

**18. Pat McGrath Labs — Steven Meisel campaigns (2019–2020)**
- Documented: Skin Fetish: Sublime Perfection foundation (2019) and Dark Star mascara (2020) shot by Steven Meisel, with high-profile casting (Naomi Campbell).
- Teaches: for prestige colour, the face and the finish are shot with fashion-level glamour, and the make-up artist's authority is the brand.
- Sources: [PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/pat-mcgrath-labs-unveils-skin-fetish-sublime-perfection-the-campaign-by-steven-meisel-300887495.html), [Beauty Packaging](https://www.beautypackaging.com/contents/view_breaking-news/2019-07-19/pat-mcgrath-labs-launches-campaign-by-steven-meisel-for-new-foundation-makeup/)

**19. Charlotte Tilbury — Old Hollywood**
- Documented: film-noir styling with pin curls, winged liner and beaded gowns (Kate Moss, Jourdan Dunn); Pillow Talk In Bloom with Sienna Miller (dir./photo Betina DuToit, shot on a farm in Kent).
- Teaches: one era code held across campaigns builds recognition; a named shade family (Pillow Talk) can carry its own world.
- Sources: [Breaking News IE](https://www.breakingnews.ie/lifestyle/kate-moss-stuns-in-first-major-beauty-campaign-since-turning-50-with-a-nod-to-marilyn-monroe-1578616.html), [Farm Locations](https://www.farmlocations.co.uk/?p=42931), [Charlotte Tilbury](https://www.charlottetilbury.com/secrets/christina-aguilera-makeup)

## G. India

**20. ASCI guidelines for skin-lightening and fairness advertising (2014)**
- Documented: ads must not show darker-skinned people as unattractive, unhappy, unsuccessful or disadvantaged; before-and-after images must keep the same expression and avoid post-production exaggeration; skin tone must not be tied to class, caste, community or profession. The guidelines are voluntary. Confirm the current version with ASCI.
- Teaches: the rule set every Indian beauty campaign has to clear, even for products that aren't "fairness" products.
- Sources: [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/asci-releases-final-guidelines-for-skin-lightening-fairness-products-ads-57077.html), [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/advertising/41683_asci-releases-advertising-guidelines-for-fairness-products), [Khaitan & Co (PDF)](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-03/ASCI%27s%20Guidelines%20for%20Advertising%20of%20Skin%20Lightening%20or%20Fairness%20Improvement%20Products.pdf)

**21. Fair & Lovely → Glow & Lovely (HUL, 2020)**
- Documented: "fair" dropped in June–July 2020, with messaging moving to glow, even tone, clarity and radiance; critics questioned whether stereotypes remained; J&J and L'Oréal made similar moves.
- Teaches: the Indian category has moved from "fair" to "glow". A campaign still built on lightening is out of step with the market and invites criticism.
- Sources: [Campaign India](https://campaignindia.in/article/hul-renames-fair-lovely-as-glow-lovely/462131), [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2020/06/25/fair-and-lovely-to-be-renamed-as-glow-and-lovely.amp.html), [Dazed](https://www.dazeddigital.com/beauty/article/49623/1/unilever-is-changing-the-name-of-its-fair-lovely-skin-care-brand)

**22. Forest Essentials — luxury Ayurveda (W+K Delhi and others)**
- Documented: "Warrior Princess" (W+K Delhi, Gang Films Paris), "The Power of Purity", an Apsara TVC (dir. Paul Minor), and "Take Your Time" (2026, slow beauty: oil infusions, fermentation, ritual).
- Teaches: heritage and ritual can sell skincare when the product is luxury and process-led. Craft and time are the visible proof.
- Sources: [Campaign India](https://www.campaignindia.in/article/forest-essentials-launches-take-your-time-slow-beauty-campaign/40p8z1gt1a46txjf1pmpzxawd0), [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/wk-delhis-warrior-princess-prepares-for-battle-in-forest-essentials-spot), [Storyboard18](https://stgcms.storyboard18.com/?p=9880)

**23. Minimalist — ingredient percentages on the front label**
- Documented: every product states its active and concentration (e.g. Niacinamide 10%); plain packaging by design; education-led growth before paid advertising; often compared to The Ordinary.
- Teaches: in India too, ingredient transparency is a visual system. The label is the claim, so it must be legible and exact.
- Sources: [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2021/09/jaipur-based-skincare-brand-minimalist-transparency-beauty), [Cosmetics Design Asia](https://www.cosmeticsdesign-asia.com/Article/2020/11/17/Clean-beauty-in-India-New-beauty-brand-Minimalist-says-education-a-key-strategic-pillar-to-drive-growth)

**24. Indian colour cosmetics and D2C skincare: SUGAR, Kay Beauty, Lakmé, Foxtale, Dot & Key, Mamaearth**
- Documented:
  - **SUGAR:** #BoldAndFree with Taapsee Pannu (2021) and #MySkinMyChoice on skin-colour bias.
  - **Kay Beauty:** with Nykaa (2019); shade ranges "curated for Indian skin tones and undertones" (20 foundation, 13 concealer shades).
  - **Lakmé:** #UnapologeticallyMé (Ogilvy Mumbai, 2023) and the 9to5 range with Aneet Padda.
  - **Foxtale:** "You Glow Different" (real, unfiltered skin) and colour-coded recall.
  - **Dot & Key:** colour-coded sunscreen packaging.
  - **Mamaearth:** a winter-glow campaign.
- Teaches: Indian beauty advertising leans on celebrity faces and empowerment narratives. Shade ranges built for Indian undertones are a selling point, and colour-coding by product line helps recall.
- Sources: [exchange4media — SUGAR #MySkinMyChoice](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/sugar-cosmetics-digital-campaign-myskinmychoice-questions-skin-colour-based-biases-95070.html), [ThePrint — SUGAR #BoldAndFree](https://theprint.in/ani-press-releases/sugar-cosmetics-launches-its-bold-and-free-campaign-featuring-taapsee-pannu/738235/), [Fashion Network — Kay Beauty concealer](https://in.fashionnetwork.com/news/Kay-beauty-steps-into-concealers-expands-skin-products-with-13-shade-line,1368845.html), [The Drum — Lakmé](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2023/02/27/indian-make-up-brand-lakme-celebrates-unapologetic-women), [afaqs — Foxtale](https://www.afaqs.com/news/mktg/colour-coded-recall-quirky-collabs-and-digital-first-marketing-foxtales-playbook-for-scale-10521398), [afaqs — Dot & Key](https://www.afaqs.com/news/advertising/dot-key-unveils-new-sunscreen-packaging-with-shanaya-kapoor-11103381)

## Avatiser's own render tests (Nov 2026)

First-hand evidence from Avatiser's own renders for the GYK and Nicobar test campaigns (`studio/projects/`):
- **holding-water (GYK, stepwell):** heritage location, warm film grade and full-length framing read as fashion editorial. The product was about 2% of the frame, the murky water read as unhygienic, and the ochre fought the aqua packaging. Rejected.
- **pool-blue (GYK, pool):** close crops, a palette from the packaging, clear water and crisp digital read as skincare. The first hero still produced an invented second product and spare lids, application at the lower eyelid (reads as eye drops, an unsafe use), garbled label text, dark cobalt capsules instead of light aqua, and a stray swimmer at the frame edge.
- **walking-pace (Nicobar, fashion):**
  - Casting by skin tone and hair alone defaulted to the wrong ethnicity; nationality, city and specific cues fixed it.
  - A palette line naming accessories ("chartreuse only on the scarf and beanie") made the generator add both to everyone.
  - Attaching a hero to a wide shot pulled the figures to hero size.
