## 1. Principles

1. **Skin and product are the subject; the location serves them.** Fashion sells a mood; beauty sells a result you can see. Every studied product campaign keeps the face, the texture or the pack close enough to read [2, 5, 8, 9, 16]. In our test, a strong location at full length made the product vanish [T].
2. **One idea, taken from a product truth.** The idea comes from what the product *is* or *does*: its texture, its ingredient, its ritual, its proof, or the people it is for (section 2). A borrowed mood with no tie to the product is decoration.
3. **The palette comes from the packaging.** Strong systems let one pack colour or material own the world: Laneige blue [10], Aesop amber [16], The Ordinary white and black [5], Glow Recipe watermelon [15]. Choose sets the packaging's colours can own [T].
4. **Unretouched skin.** Real texture is a documented brand position, not a style accident [1, 2, 24 Foxtale]. Keep pores, fine hair and real tone; never plastic or airbrushed.
5. **The label is sacred.** Packaging text must be exact, especially for ingredient-led brands where the label *is* the claim [5, 23]. It's never typed into a prompt; it's locked from the product photo [T].
6. **Range is part of the message.** For complexion and colour products, show the spectrum together [17, 24 Kay Beauty]. For Indian brands, shoot warm, olive and neutral undertones across light to deep.
7. **Claims discipline.** No lightening, no "fairness", no dark-skin-as-problem, no exaggerated before/after [20, 21]. Glow, hydration, clarity, texture and ritual are the category's current language.
8. **Show it being used, correctly.** In-use demonstration is persuasive [7], but the use must be the real, safe one: cream on the cheek, not at the lash line [T].

## 2. Where the idea comes from (six sources)

Pick one as the spine and state it in one sentence. Two can combine if one leads.

| Source | What it means | Studied examples | Typical hero frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Texture / finish** | The formula's look and feel is the claim: glaze, dew, gel, capsule, balm, matte, glitter | Rhode glazing [9], texture smears [12] | Product on skin, macro; droplets, sheen, swirl |
| **Ingredient / origin** | Where the key ingredient comes from, shown as a real world | Kiehl's [13], Innisfree Jeju [14], Glow Recipe [15], La Mer kelp [11] | The ingredient's place or form beside the product |
| **Ritual / use** | The gesture and time of using it; slowness, care, craft | Forest Essentials "Take Your Time" [22], Aesop calm-at-home [16] | Hands applying, the moment in a real room |
| **Proof / science** | Formula, concentration, dermatology, testing | The Ordinary [5], Minimalist [23], CeraVe [6], Vaseline Verified [7], Clinique/Penn [8] | Product on clean ground, label legible; tested in use |
| **People / range** | Who it is for: real skin, every tone, a social truth | Dove [1], Fenty [17], SK-II [3], SUGAR / Kay Beauty [24] | Faces together, unretouched; the spectrum in one frame |
| **Sensorial element** | One physical element that rhymes with the benefit: water for hydration, sun for SPF, night for repair | Laneige water [10], La Mer sea [11], Pool Blue [T] | Skin and pack inside that element: wet, lit, cool |

**Concept tests:**
- **Grounded:** it comes from the product or brand, not an invented story.
- **Generative:** it decides set, light and palette.
- **Visible:** you can read it without a caption.
- **Sells:** a viewer can tell what the product does from the hero alone. This test is unique to beauty.

## 3. Campaign archetypes

| Archetype | Idea | Light | Palette | Set | Product role | Face |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Clinical white** | The formula is the hero | Large soft source, shadowless or one soft shadow | White, black, one pack colour | Seamless, lab glass, plinth | Label legible, centred or on a third | None, or a calm clean-skin portrait |
| **Penn-style still life** | One product composed as art | Single controlled key, deep falloff | Pared back, 2–3 tones | Minimal props with a reason | The whole campaign | None |
| **Texture macro** | The finish is the claim | Raking side light for form; backlight for gels and translucency | Product colour on neutral or tonal ground | Glass, skin, stone, water surface | Smear, swirl, drop, capsule beside or on the pack | Lips, cheekbone or hand in close crop |
| **Glazed skin** | Skin wearing the product | Hard sun or a strong key for specular sheen; mist droplets | Tonal skin and neutrals | Minimal; water, white cotton | Small but sharp, or poured on the body | Eyes closed or a level gaze; droplets on skin |
| **Sensorial element** | Water, sun, night or air carries the benefit | Matched to the element: caustics, sun, moonlight blue | Taken from the pack | Pool, rain glass, sea, sky | Inside the element, wet and lit by it | Wet, dewy, calm |
| **Ingredient world** | Show where it comes from | Natural light of that place | The ingredient's colours | The real place or the raw ingredient | Beside the ingredient | Optional; hands harvesting or holding |
| **Apothecary / object** | The material of the pack is the art direction | Soft directional, low contrast | One material family (amber, stone, wood) | Paper, stone, books, few props | Grouped bottles, material-led | Rare; hands only |
| **Ritual at home** | The gesture and the moment | Window light, morning or night | Warm neutrals plus the pack colour | A real bathroom, dresser or bed | On the counter, mid-use | Mid-application, absorbed |
| **Range line-up** | Everyone, every tone | Even soft light that renders every tone truly | Neutral ground so the skin reads | Seamless, or one shared simple set | Shade bottles in a row or on skin | Several faces, equal weight |
| **Glamour / era** | Prestige colour as cinema | Hard key, film-noir shadow or a beauty dish | Era grade: red lip, black, gold | Studio, era interior | Compact, lipstick as jewellery | Direct gaze, a finished look |
| **Proof in use** | Tested, verified, demonstrated | Clean, documentary | Brand colours | Lab bench or real use | Being used, with result visible | Real person, real reaction |
| **Social truth film** | A cultural tension the brand takes a side on | Documentary | Real-world | Real location, real people | Often only at the end | Real expressions, testimony |

The first ten archetypes produce stills. **Proof in use** and **Social truth film** are mainly film formats. If a still campaign uses them, pair them with a product-led still.

## 4. Light for skin and product

| Source | Use it for | Prompt phrasing |
|---|---|---|
| Large soft frontal key with fill | Even, true skin; range line-ups; clinical | "large soft key from camera front-left, white fill opposite, true neutral colour" |
| Raking side light | Texture: smears, swirls, powder, skin texture | "low raking light from camera left across the cream, carving every ridge" |
| Backlight / transmitted light | Gels, serums, oils, translucent bottles, capsules glowing | "backlit through the gel, the capsules glowing" |
| Hard sun | Glow, SPF, glazed sheen, summer | "hard morning sun from high camera left, crisp specular highlights on wet skin" |
| Water caustics | Hydration, pools, glass of water | "rippling caustic light reflected from the water across skin and tile" |
| Window light | Ritual at home, morning routine | "soft window light from frame left, slow falloff" |
| Single spot / beauty dish | Prestige colour, glamour | "beauty dish overhead-front, crisp shadow under the chin" |
| Mist / droplets | Glazed and hydration finishes | "fine mist droplets beaded on skin and glass" |

**Light describes the material:**

| Material | Behaviour | Light |
|---|---|---|
| Gel, serum | Translucent, glows | Backlight, or edge light through it |
| Cream, balm | Matte to satin body, holds peaks | Raking side light |
| Oil | Liquid highlights, drips | One hard key for a crisp highlight |
| Powder, pressed shadow | Matte, crumbles | Raking light, crisp shadow |
| Glitter, metallic | Sparkles | Hard point source |
| Clear glass and plastic jars | Reflect and refract | Controlled highlights, one window shape |
| Frosted plastic | Diffuses | Soft key, avoid hotspots |
| Matte cardboard boxes | Absorb light, show print | Soft even key, so the print reads |
| Metal caps and pumps | Mirror the room | Plan the reflection; a dark or white card |

## 5. Product staging

- **Size and share.** In a product-led frame, packaging fills at least 20–30% of the frame. In a people-led hero, hand-held product should still read at thumbnail size [T].
- **Label to camera.** Front label square to the lens, or within about 30° of square. Never cropped, never covered by fingers.
- **One of each.** Only the real products of the brief, one of each unless the brief asks for multiples. Name every product and say that nothing else is in frame [T].
- **Lids and open jars.** An open jar shows the texture. Its lid sits beside it, one lid per jar.
- **Contact and scale.** Products need a contact shadow and a true size ("a 50 g jar about 7 cm across, fits in a palm").
- **Paper packaging stays dry.** Put cartons on a towel or a dry surface, never in water.
- **Props with a reason.** At most 1–2: the ingredient, a towel, a glass of water. Generic leaves, petals and rocks read as stock.
- **Texture beside the pack.** A swatch, smear, drop or capsule next to the product shows what is inside.

## 6. Texture vocabulary

Use precise nouns:
- **Creams:** swirl, peak, ribbon, smear, dollop, scoop with a spatula.
- **Gels:** bead, droplet, sheen, glaze, pool.
- **Capsules:** melted capsule, sphere, pearl, burst.
- **Oils:** drip, puddle, film.
- **Powders:** crumble, dusting, pressed pan, broken pan.
- **Lipsticks:** bullet, swatch stroke, gloss.
- **Finishes:** matte, satin, dewy, glossy, metallic, glitter, glazed.

Describe the motion when there is one: "a fingertip drags a ribbon of cream across the glass".

## 7. Skin rendering

- **Always:** visible pores, fine vellus hair, natural tone variation, small marks left in, real fine lines on mature skin. Say "unretouched".
- **Finish words:** "dewy" (healthy sheen on the high planes), "glazed" (wet-look gloss), "satin". Never "oily", "flawless", "porcelain" or "poreless".
- **Water on skin:** beaded droplets, wet strands of hair, collarbone pools.
- **Application point:** cheekbone, back of the hand, jawline, inner wrist. Never at the lash line or inside the eye area unless the product is an eye product, and then name the safe zone ("along the orbital bone, below the lashes").
- **Tone truth:** neutral colour balance so every skin tone is true. No warming or cooling the skin to flatter it, and no lightening in grading [20].

## 8. Palette strategies

- **From the pack:** the pack colour becomes the set (a blue jar in turquoise water; amber bottles on amber stone). This is the strongest rhyme in skincare [10, 16, T].
- **Pack against neutral:** the product as the only colour on white, stone or skin. Clinical and premium [5, 8].
- **Ingredient palette:** the ingredient's colours (green tea, watermelon pink, kelp gold-green) [14, 15, 11].
- **Line colour-coding:** each line owns one colour; never mix two lines' colours in one frame unless it's a range shot [10, 24 Foxtale, Dot & Key].
- **Avoid:** a generic millennial pink or a "spa beige" with no tie to the pack; warm film grades that fight a cool pack [T].

## 9. Colour cosmetics: shade, finish, swatch

- **Accurate colour first.** Use neutral light with no saturation push, and render the shade as it looks in the pan [12].
- **Swatch on skin, more than one tone.** A swatch stripe on the inner forearm or the back of the hand across at least three undertones and depths (for India: light warm, medium olive, deep warm).
- **Name the finish:** matte, satin, cream, gloss, metallic, shimmer, glitter. Light it accordingly (glitter needs a hard point source).
- **Macro beauty crops:** lips (texture of the bullet on the lip line), eyes (lid, lashes, liner wing), cheek (flush on the high plane).
- **Range shot:** shade bottles or bullets in tone order with faces in the same tone order, equal size, equal light [17].
- **Glamour is allowed:** prestige colour can borrow fashion and era codes [18, 19], but the shade must still read true.

## 10. Casting, claims and India

- **Name nationality or region, city, age and skin depth** in every prompt. Skin tone and hair alone default to the generator's assumptions [T].
- **Cast for the market**, and celebrate deep and mature skin. Never cast a darker tone as the "before" [20].
- **Banned framings:** fairness, whitening, lightening, "brighter = better" comparisons; skin tone tied to marriage, jobs, status or caste [20]. Use glow, hydration, clarity, even-looking tone, radiance [21].
- **Before/after:** avoid it. If the brief requires it: same light, same expression, no retouching beyond the product's real effect, and no tone change [20].
- **Ingredient-led brands:** the percentage on the label must stay legible and exact; it's the claim [23].
- **Celebrity faces** are common in Indian beauty [24]. With a real person, use only licensed likeness references, never a generated lookalike.

## 11. Generator failure modes (tested)

| Failure | Seen in | Prevention in the prompt | Fix after render |
|---|---|---|---|
| Garbled label text | Every packshot [T] | Never type label text; "label exactly as in the product photo" | Product-lock edit with the clean pack photo |
| Invented second product, spare lids | Pool Blue hero [T] | Name every product; "this one jar and its lid are the only products in frame" | Edit: remove extras |
| Wrong product colour (cobalt vs aqua) | Pool Blue hero [T] | Describe the colour precisely, from the photo | Lock or recolour in edit |
| Unsafe application (at the lash line) | Pool Blue hero [T] | "on the cheekbone, well below the eye" | Edit: move the hand, or re-roll |
| Wrong-ethnicity casting | Walking Pace [T] | Nationality + city + specific cues first; casting headshot | Re-cast the headshot |
| Accessories or products leaking between people | Walking Pace [T] | Don't name both people's items in a shared line; say what each person is not wearing or holding | Edit |
| Signage and lettering in the set | Walking Pace, stepwell [T] | "No signs or lettering anywhere"; describe plain surfaces | Edit: blank the sign |
| Product size drifting between frames | Pool Blue (jar size guessed) [T] | A `STRICT PRODUCT SCALE LOCK:` sentence (§14) | Re-roll with the lock; never resize in an edit |
| Wrong use context (applied in water, on wet skin, or a non-SPF product in pool sun) | Pool Blue (HBC is applied to cleansed, dry skin, morning and night) [T] | Read "How to use"; stage application exactly as directed; water only as metaphor in non-use frames | Re-direct the shot |
| Stray people at frame edges | Pool Blue hero [T] | "No other people" | Edit: remove |
| Fashion look instead of beauty | Stepwell [T] | Skincare codes (sections 1, 3–8) | Re-direct |

## 12. Anti-patterns

- A location as the hero, with the product tiny and the skin far away [T].
- Murky, dirty or stagnant water, mud, or anything unhygienic near a face product [T].
- Plastic, poreless, airbrushed skin; beauty-filter faces.
- A warm, nostalgic film grade on a fresh or cool product [T].
- Generic spa props (pebbles, orchids, bamboo, petals) with no tie to the formula.
- Floating products with no contact shadow; impossible splashes around a jar that couldn't hold them.
- More than one product line's colours in one frame, by accident.
- Two or more people's products in one frame with no clear owner.
- Typed label text; hallucinated ingredient percentages.
- Any lightening, fairness or before/after framing [20, 21].

## 13. Visual schools and light rigs

| School | Typical brands (as named in the sweep) | Light rig | Surfaces and space | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Global luxury prestige** | Dior, La Mer, SK-II, Shiseido, Estée Lauder | Broad frontal diffusion (large octabox or silk scrim) with black negative-fill flags sculpting the cheekbones; hard edge kickers on packaging | Dark reflective plinths; packaging lit like museum sculpture | Skin must still show pores; no plastic luxury |
| **Cult D2C / tactile minimalism** | Rhode, Glossier, Summer Fridays, Milk, Merit | Natural daylight with real specular highlights on skin; hard sun casting sculptural tabletop shadows | Single-hue world from the hero ingredient or pack; macro "edible" texture: glossy smears, whipped peaks, water caustics | Keep it one hue; no props without a reason |
| **High-fashion colour cosmetics** | Pat McGrath, NARS, Prada Beauty, Byredo, Valentino | Match light hardness to the finish: crisp strobe for vinyl gloss and metallics, a diffused key for velvet matte | Graphic colour contrast; true pigment on real pores | The shade must read true under the drama |
| **Apothecary / clinical derm / K-beauty** | Aesop, Diptyque, Le Labo, The Ordinary, CeraVe, Tatcha, Tamburins | Apothecary: deep raking shadows, film. Derm: flat overhead clinical light. Real optical caustics through glass | 45–70% deliberate negative space: drapery, stone, an architectural wall | Don't mix the apothecary and clinical registers |
| **Luxury haircare** | K18, Kérastase, Dyson, Oribe | Tight back-edge rim light for mirror shine on strands; fast shutter | Kinetic strand separation, real airflow, liquid physics | Hair physics must be real; no impossible swirls |
| **Indian luxury Ayurveda** | Forest Essentials, Kama Ayurveda, Sabyasachi × Estée Lauder, Indē Wild | Warm golden-hour light or sandstone-jaali lattice light, calibrated for South Asian undertones | Terracotta plaster, rosewood, kansa brass, Chanderi or khadi fabric; restrained botanicals (a few saffron threads, one lotus petal) | Heritage only where the brand is heritage-led [22]; product still large, skin still close [T] |
| **Modern Indian D2C** *(added here, not in the sweep)* | Minimalist, Foxtale, Dot & Key, Kay Beauty, Fae Beauty | Clean directional daylight or a soft studio key; neutral colour balance so every undertone stays true | Line colour-coding, legible actives on labels [23, 24] | Don't impose heritage codes on clinical or colour-coded brands |

**One light source per related set of frames:** name one physically plausible source, its angle and the time of day, and match its hardness to the brand's proof (hard for glow and gloss, soft for matte and shade truth). Keep it fixed across related frames.

**Skin-tone note:** the sweep warns against flat frontal ring light and cool grey flash on South Asian and melanin-rich skin, and recommends directional light with controlled falloff. Keep the white balance neutral so tones stay true (§7): "warm light" means the light's direction and quality, not grading the skin warmer.

**Light and lens shorthand.** These are the light and lens qualities the sweep cited from named photographers, written as physical descriptions. Use the description, never a name.

| Quality | Physical description to prompt |
|---|---|
| Honest documentary beauty | Large frontal diffused key at eye level, direct gaze, unretouched pores and fine lines, 85 mm |
| Crisp graphic 90s strobe | Hard strobe from high front, crisp shadow under the jaw, saturated architectural colour blocks, contoured sheen on lips and cheeks |
| Intimate raw daylight | Warm directional window or late sun, close handheld 50 mm, dewy bare skin, a hand touching the face |
| Sculptural beam | A narrow beam of directional light slicing deep shadow across the face, the rest falling to black |
| Painterly daylight with glass | Soft natural sun through coloured or textured glass, casting caustics beside sculptural botanicals |
| Rich warm saturation on deep skin | Warm-direction key, rich saturated colour around the subject, deep skin rendered luminous and true |
| Precision tabletop macro | Flagged hard light on a macro still life, water caustics, jewel-like refraction through glass, focus-stacked |

## 14. Product scale lock table

A white-background product photo carries no sense of size. Every prompt showing packaging needs a `STRICT PRODUCT SCALE LOCK:` sentence built from this table. Measure the real SKU where you can: these are typical sizes, and the true dimensions win.

| Format | Typical height | Hand grip | Face / prop anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 ml eye serum / lip oil | 7.5–8.5 cm | Thumb plus two fingers; the body spans two stacked fingers (about 4.5 cm) | About 35% of face height (chin to brow); shorter than a lipstick tube |
| 30 ml dropper / face oil | 9.5–10.5 cm | Full three-finger wrap around a ~5.5 cm glass body; collar and bulb rise ~4 cm above the index finger; never a two-finger pinch | About 45% of face height; on a table, the glass body matches an espresso cup |
| 50 ml perfume bottle | 10.5–12 cm | Full four-finger wrap around a ~7.5 cm body; the cap rises ~3.5 cm above the index knuckle | About 50% of face height; reaches halfway up a teapot |
| 50 g / 50 ml cream jar | 5–7 cm tall, 6–8 cm across | Sits in the palm with the fingers curled around its side, or the lid unscrewed between thumb and fingers | About a third of face height; the width of a teacup |
| 100 ml body oil / mist | 14–16 cm | Full-palm wrap around the middle third; the top third rises above the hand | About 70% of face height; taller than a coffee mug |
| 200–250 ml wash / shampoo / scrub tub | 18–21 cm (bottle) | Full palm or a two-hand cradle; taller than the hand span | About full head height (chin to hairline) for bottles |

## 15. Eight shot archetypes

| # | Archetype | Frame | What it shows | Key physics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Architectural light portrait | 4:5 or 9:16 | The person in the campaign's room, holding the pack with a locked, natural grip | Window or lattice light throwing a fixed pattern or caustics across skin and clothes |
| 2 | Museum-sculpture macro still life | 4:5, no face | The pack as an object: solo, or one upright and one angled, on wet stone or a dark reflective plinth | Hard edge kickers, micro-condensation, a frozen spill or cream smear; macro, f/11, focus-stacked |
| 3 | Overhead flat-lay | 4:5, no face | The pack among routine props that anchor scale (sleep mask, jewellery, gua sha, earphones, a notebook) on a tray or stone slab | True 90° overhead; one light; real contact shadows |
| 4 | Outdoor daylight with skin swatch | 4:5 | The face outdoors in golden light, a visible swatch on the cheek (oil bead, cream smear, flush, hydrogel), the pack near the chin | Real wind in hair, sun specular on the swatch, unretouched pores |
| 5 | Face-free tactile hold | 4:5 | Collarbone-to-knee or hands only, a rich textile (ribbed wool, khadi, washed linen) or wet stone, skin pressing slightly around the pack | Natural grip, soft compression, fibre detail |
| 6 | Motion-contrast | 4:5 or 9:16 | The subject razor-sharp with the pack at chest height while foreground fabric, hair or passers-by blur | Slow shutter on the movement only; 35 mm, fine grain |
| 7 | Monochrome or chiaroscuro hydration | 4:5 | Freshly misted bare skin, crisp specular micro-droplets, black and white or a deep-shadow beam | One hard source, deep falloff |
| 8 | Clinical lab proof | 4:5, no face | The pack with clear lab glassware, suspended droplets, a backlit lightbox, generous negative space | Flat overhead or back light; clean optical refraction |

## 16. Motion rules (Seedance)

- **Start frame:** the shot's approved, label-locked still (`@image_1`), nothing else.
- **Camera:** one move only (e.g. a locked tripod with a slow 3 cm push-in). Never stack moves.
- **Light:** the light pattern on the set stays fixed while the subject moves through it, so light glides across skin and glass.
- **Three beats over 8 s:** 0–3 s a slow breath and a natural blink; 3–6 s one small motivated gesture with the product; 6–8 s settle into the end state. Never write "frozen" or "still".
- **Sound:** room tone only, no music or dialogue.
- **Guardrails:** forbid product resizing, label drift, identity drift, jitter and flicker.
- **Render:** draft at 480p, check, then render finals at 720p or higher. 100–150 words.

## 17. Colour directions (a fallback only)

The sweep proposes trend directions for 2026–27. Use one **only when the packaging has no strong colour of its own**; the pack's palette always wins (§8). The trend names aren't sourced.
- **Soft Butter:** cream, honey, oils.
- **Dreamy Cerulean:** water, droplets, glass, hydration.
- **Moon Lilac:** pearlescent translucency.
- **Cherry Glaze:** glossy crimson or berry.
- **Silver Shift:** chrome, stainless steel, mirror.
- **Warm Terracotta and Amber.**
